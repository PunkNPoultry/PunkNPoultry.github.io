Back in 2019, we were fresh-faced, newly minted rabbit breeders having the time of our lives. Cute kids. Cute bunnies. Cute little farm. And a freezer full of delicious, lean meat we'd raised ourselves. Love it.

The rabbits were the perfect complement to our laying hens, meat sheep, and fatty lard hogs. They were easy to keep, easy to feed, easy to butcher, and, quite frankly, easy to eat.

Then came 2020.

While COVID reared its ugly head and much of humanity hunkered down and bleached its groceries, we found ourselves knee-deep in meat animals. Something had to give. Keeping everyone fed — including the animals feeding us — was getting complicated. Supply chains were a mess, feed was harder to come by, and the whole country seemed determined to ingest horse dewormers and catch a potentially deadly virus.

The rabbits were the easiest to let go. So we did.

Once the last batch was tucked away in the freezer, life moved on. Lockdowns ended, vaccines arrived, and the world slowly started putting itself back together.

And the rabbits? Well, they fell off the radar. Rabbits became a distant memory.

But apparently, bunnies are a little like Ennis and Jack: "I can't quit you."

So here we are again. \|

Our three TAMUK composite rabbits are just about the sweetest, cutest little things on the farm. (Don't tell the kids. Or the call ducks. We don't need any more competition for affection around here.)

Unlike the Californians and New Zealands we raised before, TAMUK rabbits are Texas natives and Aggies. Developed at Texas A\&M University–Kingsville in the mid-1980s, they're known for their heat tolerance, practical size, and suitability for raising in our hot Texas climate. In other words, they're a pretty natural fit for our little operation.

So, we're back in the rabbit business. We've done this before, but apparently, we needed a few years to forget why we stopped.

The freezer isn't going to fill itself, and in these trying times, we're going to have to work a little harder to fill it.

Three rabbits, a few years older, and hopefully a little wiser. Let's see how this goes.
