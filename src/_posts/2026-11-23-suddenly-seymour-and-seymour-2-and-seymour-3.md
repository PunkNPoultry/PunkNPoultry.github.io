There are times in life when you look back on a choice you made and say “Ok, self. That was a really shitty decision. And now you are stuck with the consequences.” Most of the time you can just pick up the pieces of your mess, pay the piper, and move on.

But sometimes you are faced with a life altering change. The care and responsibility of another living creature is suddenly thrust upon you.

I’m talking about baby rabbits y’all. Because apparently, we didn't have enough going on.

As you may know, we have a nice little breeder colony of TAMUK meat rabbits to keep the freezer stocked. But what you may not know is that our son decided he wanted pet bunnies, and we acquiesced. Specifically, he wanted a Holland Lop dwarf rabbit.

We found someone online looking to rehome her male bunny. And, as an added bonus, she'd also throw in his "friend" — a Blanc de Hotot mix she assured us was also male.

What could possibly go wrong?

Clearly, a lot. A whole lot, as it turns out.

We took them for a vet check, bought a large rabbit hutch/pen, and figured they'd get along just fine since they were already best friends. We stocked them with toys, hay, feed, and plenty of love, tucked them in each night, and went about our business. All was well. (Or so we thought.)

Then one night we returned home and there were no longer two active bunnies, there were four (and one that was decidedly NOT active). The new arrivals were beautiful little balls of black-and-gray fluff that looked an awful lot like the dust sprites in a Hayao Miyazaki movie.

Turns out these two adult rabbits *are* friends … with benefits. Our Hotot “boy” was in fact working with girl parts. So we separated them immediately, and went on with life, now with added cuteness. The kids, who clearly grew up on musical theater, named the babies “[Suddenly Seymour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mkMtq8qarnY)(s)”. We made sure the two “friends” could not get even friendlier between their two cages. And we thought we’d [fixed the glitch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BUE0PPQI3is).

Until now.

When the kids, came down holding this:

<%= render Figure.new(src: resized_image_path("images/baby-bunny.jpg", width: 800), caption: "Moar babies")%>

Apparently, the Hotot got pregnant almost immediately after giving birth.

 We knew this could happen theoretically, as mating actually triggers ovulation in rabbits. But we had no idea she was pregnant (again), since we separated immediately after finding the first litter.

<%= render Figure.new(src: resized_image_path("images/MaleNotMale.png", width: 800), caption: "Moar babies")%>

So now, here we sit ... seven pet bunnies deep. Suddenly Seymour became a full-blown musical production that we don’t remember buying tickets for.

Remember that shitty decision I mentioned earlier? Yeah. There is a sequel.
